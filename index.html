Project Overview

Project RAPTOR (Rocket Apogee Payload Transition to Optimal Return) is an electrical engineering capstone project conducted over two full academic semesters (Fall 2026 through Spring 2027). Our five-member Electrical Engineering team has completed initial aerodynamic lift/drag studies, 3D printed kinematics verification, OpenRocket trajectory simulations, and composite airframe layup fabrication.

The launch vehicle carries an internally folded autonomous glider to an apogee of 1,500 to 2,000 ft AGL (capable up to 4,000 ft AGL) at the Tripoli Phoenix launch site. At apogee, an e-match-actuated CO2 cold-gas canister releases the nosecone and glider without thermal hazards. Following deployment, the glider rotates its 30-inch wing into flight position and executes actively stabilized, controlled glide flight back to a designated recovery area while the rocket recovers safely under dual-deployment parachutes.

RAPTOR Mini: Design Evolution & Aerodynamics

Airframe Architectural Evolution Across Development

Version 0 - Initial Proof of Concept | Proof-of-Concept CAD & Swivel Mechanism Evolution
Proof-of-Concept Baseline

Version 0 served as our initial proof of concept, establishing the baseline 30.0-inch high-wing geometry and internal packaging envelope to fit inside the 7.51-inch rocket airframe. The initial Version 0 CAD models utilized metal thrust ball bearings in the central wing rotation joint to evaluate rotation stops and mechanical clearances.

Version 1 - Full-Scale 3D Printed Prototype & Transition to Updated Nylon Washer System | Validated in Ansys CFD & MATLAB Aerodynamic Studies
Dihedral Winglet Integration & Transition to Updated Nylon Washer System

Version 1 introduced upturned aerodynamic winglets to attenuate wingtip vortices and minimize induced drag during glide descent. To eliminate bearing binding, reduce parasitic weight, and improve deployment reliability, the heavy metal ball bearings from the proof of concept were replaced with the updated low-friction nylon washer swivel system. Full-scale physical prototypes were 3D printed to verify wing rotation stops, structural spar alignment, and stowed clearance inside the 7.51-inch launch tube. All baseline Ansys CFD streamline simulations and MATLAB aerodynamic studies below were performed on the RAPTOR Mini Version 1 geometry.

Version 2 - Current CAD with Cooling Vents & Control Surfaces | Active Foaming Bambu Lab ASA Aero Revision
Integrated Avionics Cooling Vents, Nylon Washer Pivot & Aileron Control Surfaces

Version 2 is our current flight-ready CAD iteration featuring integrated NACA-style cooling air vents to regulate internal electronics and Walksnail VTX temperatures, an updated nylon washer swivel retention system, sculpted dihedral winglets, and functional trailing-edge control surfaces.

Note: Additional internal servo linkage channels and wire routing refinements are being finalized as Version 2 moves into full CFD verification and Bambu Lab ASA Aero fabrication.

Materials, Bambu Lab ASA Aero & Propulsion Integration

While our initial prototype was printed in standard PETG to verify mechanical clearances, solid filament has a high density that increases wing loading. For our flight airframe, the team is manufacturing the glider using Bambu Lab ASA Aero active-foaming filament paired with carbon-fiber structural spars:

  • Bambu Lab ASA Aero Active-Foaming Filament: Specially formulated for lightweight UAV and RC aircraft structures, Bambu Lab ASA Aero foams as it is extruded at elevated nozzle temperatures, expanding internally to form a closed-cell microstructure. By tuning print temperature and flow rate, airframe weight is reduced by 50% to 60% compared to solid filaments while maintaining crisp outer mold line accuracy.
  • Superior UV, Weather & Thermal Resistance: Unlike standard PLA or LW-PLA which warp rapidly under solar heating, Bambu Lab ASA Aero offers exceptional UV stability and high heat resistance, preventing deformation on the flight line in the Arizona desert.
  • Carbon Fiber Spar Backbone: Continuous carbon fiber tubes run longitudinally down the centerline of the fuselage and through the main wing root to provide high bending and torsional rigidity during apogee deployment and high-speed pull-out.
  • Rear Pusher Propeller System: A rear-mounted brushless pusher motor with folding propeller blades provides forward thrust for extended loiter and controlled return to the recovery zone.

Pitch Moment, Longitudinal Stability & Battery Placement Analysis (30 mph)

To guarantee stable autonomous recovery after apogee ejection, longitudinal static stability was analyzed by evaluating the aircraft's pitching moment ($M$) at 30 mph across both $\alpha = 4^\circ$ and $\alpha = 12^\circ$. As angle of attack increases from $4^\circ$ to $12^\circ$, total lift increases by $+1.471\text{ N}$ while the pitching moment becomes more negative by $\Delta M = -0.1362\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ (from $-0.2278\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ to $-0.3640\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$). Because $\frac{\partial M}{\partial \alpha} < 0$, the aircraft produces a natural nose-down restoring moment whenever disturbed upward by wind gusts, confirming positive longitudinal static stability when the Center of Gravity (CG) is positioned forward of the Aerodynamic Neutral Point (NP).

RAPTOR: Launch Vehicle Airframe, Fabrication & Trajectory

Custom 7.51-Inch High-Powered Rocket

Because housing and deploying a 30-inch-wingspan drone exceeds standard kit rockets, the team engineered a custom high-power rocket airframe. The vehicle features a 7.51-inch outer diameter (with a 20.0-inch total fin span) and stands 110.0 inches (9.17 feet) tall, designed to carry the RAPTOR Mini to an apogee of 1,500 to 2,000 ft AGL and recover safely under dual-deployment parachutes.

Custom Composite Layup & Laser-Cut Aerodynamic Fins

While commercial 7.5-inch rocket kits are manufactured and sold, our team deliberately chose not to purchase a pre-assembled commercial kit. Instead, to customize our internal payload bay dimensions and maintain tight budget control, we ordered standalone 7.51-inch heavy kraft cardboard tubes that we are reinforcing ourselves using wet-layup fiberglass fabric and epoxy resin.

Laser-Cut Composite Fins: For the control fins, we used a laser cutter to cut very thin precision wood cores, and then laid fiberglass sheets over the wood with epoxy. This composite sandwich construction produces extremely stiff, lightweight fins capable of handling high aerodynamic loads and motor thrust without deflection.

The RAPTOR CO2 Cold-Gas Ejection & Internal Rail System

Traditional pyrotechnic black powder ejection charges produce intense flame, superheated gas, and corrosive soot that would scorch and deform thin 3D-printed aerodynamic drone skin. To ensure clean deployment, our team selected the RAPTOR CO2 Ejection System (precision CNC-machined aluminum and stainless steel unit).

Deployment Mechanics: The system is actuated using electric matches (e-matches) triggered by the Blue Raven flight computer upon reaching apogee. The e-match initiates a micro-charge that drives a piercing pin into the CO2 cartridge. The instant release of pressurized cold gas acts against an internal piston/baffle to push the RAPTOR Mini smoothly along an internal rail system out of the rocket payload bay into free flight without thermal exposure.

Building Progress: Fiberglass Airframe Layup

In early November 2026, the team began full-scale composite manufacturing of the 7.51-inch rocket airframe. To achieve a uniform, high-strength fiberglass layup over the 54-inch and 30-inch heavy kraft body tubes, we constructed a horizontal PVC pipe rotation mandrel supported on sawhorses over a protective tarp. The team wrapped full-length fiberglass cloth around each airframe section, mixed two-part laminating epoxy resin, and worked collaboratively with squeegees and rollers to saturate the weave and eliminate air pockets. This hand-laid composite shell provides the hoop strength and axial rigidity required to withstand high-thrust K-class motor boost and apogee CO2 deployment forces.
