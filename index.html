Semester 1: Prototyping & Simulation

Project Overview

Project RAPTOR (Rocket Apogee Payload Transition to Optimal Return) is an electrical engineering capstone project conducted over two full academic semesters (Fall 2026 through Spring 2027). As of October 2026, our five-member Electrical Engineering team has completed initial aerodynamic lift/drag studies, 3D printed kinematics verification, OpenRocket trajectory simulations, and structural airframe procurement.

The mission challenge is to launch a custom 7.51″ airframe carrying an internally folded autonomous glider to an apogee of 1,500 to 2,000 ft AGL (capable up to 4,000 ft AGL) at the Tripoli Phoenix launch site. At apogee, an e-match-actuated CO2 cold-gas canister releases the nosecone and glider without thermal hazards. Following deployment, the glider rotates its 30-inch wing into flight position and executes actively stabilized, controlled glide flight back to a designated recovery area while the rocket recovers safely under dual-deployment parachutes.

Raptor Mini: Design Evolution & Aerodynamics

Airframe Architectural Evolution Across Development

Version 0 (V0) — Conceptual Baseline & Benchtop Prototyping | Flat-Wing Baseline & Mechanism Verification
Swivel Joint Cutaway & Initial PETG Prototype Rig

The conceptual gray baseline established our geometric constraints: a 30.0-inch flat, straight high-wing profile and an inverted V-tail empennage. The CAD cross-section highlights the central bearing retention pin and locking collar. Physical full-scale models were 3D printed in PETG to evaluate mechanism kinematics, rotation stops, and clearance within the 7.51-inch launch tube in both deployed and stowed states.

Version 1 (V1) — Aerodynamically Refined CAD Model | Validated in Ansys CFD & Trajectory Simulations
Dihedral Winglet Integration & Active Foaming Profile

Version 1 introduced aerodynamic winglets with an upturned dihedral to attenuate circular tip vortices and minimize induced drag during loiter glide. All preliminary lift/drag and Ansys CFD flow simulations documented on this site were executed using the Raptor Mini V1 geometry. The airframe was engineered for active-foaming ASA Aero filament, integrating continuous internal channels for longitudinal carbon-fiber spar tubes.

Version 2 (V2) — Active Control Surfaces & Pointed Wingtips | Flight-Ready Revision
Trailing-Edge Aileron Integration & Sculpted Wingtip Geometry

In the Raptor Mini V2, functional trailing-edge ailerons were added and the wingtips were pointed to refine roll authority and boundary-layer separation during descent.

Note regarding internal routing: While the outer aerodynamic surfaces and control planes are modeled, concealed linkage tunnels and internal wire routing for the flight controller are not modeled into V2 yet, and will be added in upcoming CAD releases.

Many new aerodynamic, mechanical, and internal packaging designs are coming soon!

Materials, Structural Backbone & Propulsion Integration

While our V0 prototype was printed in PETG to evaluate assembly clearances, PETG's high density yields excess wing loading. For our flight-ready iteration, the team is transitioning to ASA Aero (active foaming Acrylonitrile Styrene Acrylate) paired with structural composite reinforcers:

  • Carbon Fiber Spar Backbone: To provide rigid bending and torsional stiffness under high aerodynamic loading, we are running carbon fiber tubes down the middle/centerline of the fuselage and through the main wing root. This prevents flexure during deployment and high-speed glide transitions.
  • Rear Pusher Propeller System: The team plans to add a propeller mounted at the rear/back of the Raptor Mini (powered by a SunnySky brushless motor with folding pusher blades) to provide forward propulsion for extended loiter and controlled navigation back to the recovery coordinates.
  • ~60% Weight Reduction: ASA Aero utilizes a heat-activated foaming agent during extrusion, reducing airframe density drastically while retaining structural rigidity.
  • Extreme UV & Heat Deflection: Unlike PLA or standard foams that soften in desert heat, ASA Aero withstands temperatures exceeding 90°C and resists intense UV degradation during field testing in Wickenburg, Arizona.
  • Zero-Support Geometry Redesign: Engineered with 45° self-supporting internal overhangs to eliminate slicer support dependency.

Launch Vehicle: Airframe & Trajectory Simulations

Custom 7.51-Inch High-Powered Rocket

Because housing and deploying a 30-inch-wingspan drone exceeds commercial kit rockets, the team engineered a custom high-power rocket airframe using OpenRocket. The vehicle features a 7.51-inch (19.1 cm) outer diameter and stands 8.16 feet (249 cm / 98 inches) tall, designed to carry the Raptor Mini to an apogee of 1,500 to 2,000 ft AGL and recover safely under dual-deployment parachutes.

Custom Composite Layup & Laser-Cut Aerodynamic Fins

While commercial 7.5-inch rocket kits are manufactured and sold, our team deliberately chose not to purchase a pre-assembled commercial kit. Instead, to customize our internal payload bay dimensions and maintain tight budget control, we ordered standalone 7.51-inch heavy kraft cardboard tubes that we are reinforcing ourselves using wet-layup fiberglass fabric and epoxy resin.

Laser-Cut Composite Fins: For the control fins, we used a laser cutter to cut very thin precision wood cores, and then laid fiberglass sheets over the wood with epoxy. This composite sandwich construction produces extremely stiff, lightweight fins capable of handling supersonic shock flutter and high motor thrust without aerodynamic deflection.

The RAPTOR CO2 Cold-Gas Ejection & Internal Rail System

Traditional pyrotechnic black powder ejection charges produce intense flame, superheated gas, and corrosive soot that would scorch and deform thin 3D-printed aerodynamic drone skin. To ensure clean deployment, our team selected the RAPTOR CO2 Ejection System (precision CNC-machined aluminum and stainless steel unit).

Deployment Mechanics: The system is actuated using electric matches (e-matches) triggered by the Blue Raven flight computer upon reaching apogee. The e-match initiates a micro-charge that drives a piercing pin into the CO2 cartridge. The instant release of pressurized cold gas acts against an internal baffle to push the Raptor Mini smoothly along an internal rail system out of the rocket payload bay into free flight without thermal exposure.
